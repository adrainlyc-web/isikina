Responsible Gaming

Gambling should be entertainment, not a way to make money or solve money problems. This page explains how to keep it that way when you play at Isikina's Papua New Guinea online casino.

You must be 18 years or older to use Isikina. If you're under 18, please don't message us, deposit or play. If you're an adult, don't let anyone under 18 use your WhatsApp, your phone or your 918Kaya game ID.

Set a Budget Before You Play

  • Only play with money you can afford to lose. Never use money meant for food, school fees, rent, bills or family needs.
  • Decide your limit first. Pick an amount for the week or month before you deposit, and stick to it even after a win or a loss.
  • Don't chase losses. Depositing more to win back what you lost usually makes things worse.
  • Never borrow to gamble. That includes loans, credit from shops, or money from friends and family.
  • Remember the odds. Casino games are built so the house wins over time. No pattern or lucky streak changes that.
  • Treat bonuses carefully. A bonus comes with wagering requirements, so read the terms before you deposit. A bonus is not a reason to deposit more than you planned.

Warning Signs of a Problem

Take it seriously if you notice any of these:

  • Spending more money or time on gambling than you planned
  • Thinking about gambling most of the day
  • Gambling to escape stress, worry or sadness
  • Lying to family or friends about how much you play
  • Borrowing money or missing bills to keep playing
  • Feeling anxious, angry or guilty after playing
  • Being unable to stop, even when you want to

How to Take a Break or Stop

You can stop at any time, and you don't have to explain why. These steps are in your own hands:

  • Stop depositing. Don't send any more money.
  • Remove the temptation. Delete the 918Kaya app and mute, archive or block the Isikina chat. You can also unfollow the Facebook Page and leave the WhatsApp Channel to stop seeing promotions.
  • Take a set break. Even a week away helps you see how much you were playing.
  • Tell someone. Talk to a family member, a friend, a church leader or a counsellor you trust. Gambling problems are easier to deal with once someone else knows.
  • Protect your money. Ask someone you trust to hold your cash or help manage your spending for a while.

Getting Help

If gambling is causing problems for you or someone close to you, please talk to someone you trust, such as a family member, a church or community leader, or a health worker or counsellor in your area. You don't have to handle it alone, and asking for help is a sign of strength.
